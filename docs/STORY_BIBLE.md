# The Mist Village — Story Bible (Part 1: "The Wrong Accusation")

Draft 1, 2026-10-23. The decided spine is in [GAME_DESIGN.md](GAME_DESIGN.md) and the rules in [GAME_RULES.md](GAME_RULES.md).
Everything here not marked **(decided)** is a proposal to change freely. Tamil lines are a first draft and need a
native speaker's pass before recording.

**Rules for the writing:**
- Every person, company, crime, bank, police station and court is fictional. The areas and roads are real Coimbatore.
- No gore: the murder is never shown, only its traces.
- The player is never asked to break the law to move the story on.
- Dialogue exists in Tamil and English from the start, and the player chooses (decided).

---

## 1. Logline
A careful young driver in Coimbatore is promoted to be his employer's assistant. A week later his employer is dead,
and every trace (his car, his phone, his cash) points at the man he trusted most. Out on bail, signing at the
police station every evening and forbidden to leave the district, Karthik has to drive his own city to prove where
he really was.

**Themes:** trust and how it's used; an ordinary man's honesty against a system that has already decided; the
city as a witness (roads, times, cameras, tea shops); the hills on the horizon as the life he can't reach yet.

## 2. Characters

### Karthik (the player), 28
- Driver to R. Raghavan for four years; promoted in Act 1 to assistant (decided).
- Lives in a rented two-room house in **Ganapathy** with his mother and sister. His father, a bus conductor, died
  six years ago.
- Careful, polite and proud of his clean driving record: he has never had a challan. He never wears a cap; his
  sister jokes that it ruins his hair, and this becomes evidence.
- **Wants:** Divya through college, his mother's house roof fixed, one day a taxi of his own.
- **Flaw:** he trusts easily and talks freely about his day, and that becomes the leak.
- **Rides:** a ten-year-old grey scooter to work. He drives Raghavan's white SUV, which stays at Raghavan's house
  overnight.

### Lakshmi (Amma), 52
- Karthik's mother. Does tailoring from home and knows every neighbour.
- Believes in Karthik without question, but the gossip breaks her a little at a time.
- She is the player's home base: meals, worry and the family photos (evidence E14).

### Divya, 19
- Karthik's sister, second-year computer engineering at **Velliangiri College of Technology** in Peelamedu (fictional).
- Quick, funny and teases him. Her fees (₹42,000, due Friday) are why Karthik takes night delivery jobs, and why
  the police find a motive.
- In Act 4 she helps with phones, apps and CCTV formats.

### R. Raghavan (Raghavan sir), 61: the victim
- Founder of **Raghavan Developers**, a respected and old-fashioned land developer, with an office in **Gandhipuram**
  and a house in **Race Course**.
- Fair to his people. He treats Karthik almost like a son and calls him "thambi".
- He is building **The Mist Village**, a small 8-cottage resort above **Kotagiri**: his retirement dream. He keeps
  its photo on his desk (decided: the family gives it to Karthik at the end).
- **The conflict:** he refuses to sign the **Green Valley Layout**, a project on farmland at the fictional village
  of **Pudhur Vayal** off the Thondamuthur road. The farmers were tricked into selling at a fifth of the price, and
  he wants to pay them fairly first. Murdered off-screen (decided).

### Meenakshi, 58, and Priya, 31: Raghavan's family
- **Meenakshi**, his wife, is a quiet, devout retired teacher. She is the one who finally believes Karthik.
- **Priya**, his daughter, is a doctor at a city hospital. At first she is sure Karthik killed her father and hates
  him openly. In Act 4 she finds her father's handwritten note (E09) and starts to doubt.
- Together they give Karthik The Mist Village in the end (decided).

### Suresh Kumar, 46: the business partner and the real killer (decided)
- Joined Raghavan Developers ten years ago and owns 40% of it. Charming and generous with small favours.
- Always asks about your family.
- Deeply in debt to a city money-lender over a failed apartment project. Green Valley would save him, and Raghavan
  is in the way.
- **Method:** uses Karthik's new assistant role (the car, the work phone and the cash) to build the frame. Sets up
  the meeting at night, and is the one waiting at the site.
- **Tells:** a habit of turning his ring; calls Karthik "boss" jokingly; parks his black sedan nose-out "for a quick
  getaway, ha ha".

### Pandi, 34: Suresh's man
- A driver and fixer for the money-lender, lent to Suresh. Built like Karthik and wears a cap and a dark jacket.
- He sent the text, drove the SUV and returned it.
- Weak link: he is paid less than promised. In Act 6 he turns approver against Suresh.

### Bala, 29: Karthik's best friend
- A mechanic with a two-bay workshop in Ganapathy. They have been friends since school, and he is loud and loyal.
- **The second twist (decided):** Suresh brought the SUV to Bala's workshop for service two weeks before the story
  and asked for a spare key "for the office". Since then Suresh has chatted with Bala over tea, asking easy
  questions ("where's Karthik taking sir tomorrow?"), and Bala answered without a thought.
- He never knew. When he understands, he is crushed, and his testimony (E11, E12) becomes key.

### Inspector Murugesan, 50
- Station House Officer, **Nagar Police Station, Gandhipuram** (fictional; "NAGAR POLICE" as on the checkpoints in
  the game).
- Not corrupt, just sure. He has seen a hundred cases where the driver did it.
- He watches Karthik's signings; his suspicion is the bail-strike meter made human.
- Turns in Act 6, when the evidence is undeniable.

### Constable Selvi, 38
- At the station desk where Karthik signs. Says little, notices everything. She thinks the case is too neat.
- From Act 4 she quietly helps: an address, a warning before a raid, a look at the camera log.

### Advocate Sundaram, 66
- Semi-retired criminal lawyer and an old friend of Karthik's late father. Takes the case for a token fee.
- Slow, careful and funny. Gets bail in Act 3. Teaches the player how evidence works ("a fact needs two legs").

### Others
- **Muniyandi, 63:** night watchman of the Gandhipuram office building. Saw the man in the cap (E06).
- **Revathi, 26:** receptionist at Raghavan Developers. Scared of Suresh, but helps Priya find the file (E09).
- **Joseph, 55:** the office accountant. Knows the books don't add up but keeps quiet until Act 6.
- **Ashwin, 27:** IT worker in Saravanampatti, the late-night delivery customer. His doorbell camera saw Karthik
  (E05).
- **Sekar, 45:** runs a tea shop on the Thondamuthur road near Pudhur Vayal and saw two cars that night (E08).
- **Ponnusamy, 67:** leader of the Pudhur Vayal farmers. Raghavan was going to pay them; he thinks Karthik stole
  their money, until Act 4.
- **Aunty next door (Kamala):** the neighbourhood's voice of gossip, and later of apology.

## 4. The crime: what really happened

### Background
- **Two weeks before Day 1:** Suresh takes the SUV to Bala's workshop for service and has a spare key cut, "for the
  office" (E11). Bala writes it on the job card.
- **Over those two weeks:** Suresh befriends Bala over evening tea and learns Karthik's routines (E12).

### The full game's Act 1 (Days 1–3; the murder is on the night of Day 3)
- **Day 1:** Raghavan promotes Karthik to assistant. Suresh gives him a **work phone** registered in Karthik's name
  ("company rules, boss").
- **Day 2:** Karthik asks Raghavan for an advance on his salary for Divya's fees. Raghavan gives ₹20,000 of the
  ₹42,000 (the police later call this motive). Karthik sees Raghavan refuse to sign the Green Valley papers in front
  of Suresh.
- **Day 3, 10:30:** on Raghavan's written instruction, Karthik withdraws **₹4,00,000** in cash at the cooperative
  bank (E03) and hands it to Raghavan in a brown office bag. Raghavan writes in the land file: *"Pudhur Vayal
  farmers: pay fair difference. ₹4 L advance Friday. Will not sign GVL until paid."* (E09)

### Why it points at Karthik (the police's case)
1. **The message:** the text that lured Raghavan out came from Karthik's phone (E01).
2. **The car:** the car was seen on the way to the site, with a driver Karthik's size (E02).
3. **The keys:** only Karthik drives that car, and he knows where the key hangs.
4. **The cash:** Karthik himself withdrew ₹4 lakh that day, and it's gone (E03).
5. **The motive:** he needed ₹42,000 for fees and had asked for an advance the day before.
6. **The alibi:** his own app's GPS says Saravanampatti, but "phones can be carried by friends" (E04, unreliable on
   its own).

### How the truth is proven (Act 6)
The case is won by showing it was **impossible for Karthik** and **possible only for Suresh**:
- **Karthik's timeline:** Gandhipuram at 21:50 → Saravanampatti at 22:20 (doorbell video E05). The SUV was at
  Lawley Road at 22:12, on the opposite side of the city. On real roads at night that's 28 to 35 minutes apart, so
  he could not be both (the journey reconstruction).
- **The phone:** the phone that sent E01 was in the office (E07: building video at 21:40–21:58), and the man there
  used Suresh's key card.
- **The cap:** Karthik never wears a cap (family photos E14, colleagues). The driver in E02 wears one, and so does
  the man in E07.
- **The duplicate key:** cut on Suresh's order (E11).
- **The routines:** Suresh learnt Karthik's routines from Bala (E12).
- **The cash:** ₹4 lakh deposited in cash two days later into "Om Sakthi Traders", a front of Suresh's money-lender
  (E10).
- **The second car:** Suresh's black sedan at the site (E08).
- **The motive:** Raghavan's note (E09) shows the cash was for the farmers and that he refused to sign. Suresh's
  debts give him the motive.
- **The confession:** Pandi, cornered by phone records (E13), turns approver.

## 6. The six acts (full game)
The days are game days, at 48 real minutes each (GAME_RULES.md §1).

### Act 1, Ordinary life (Days 1–3)
- **Story:** the routine of mornings at Race Course and the Gandhipuram office; the promotion (Day 1); the advance
  (Day 2); the refused signature (Day 2); the withdrawal (Day 3); the phone left at the office (Day 3); the night
  delivery (Day 3).
- **Gameplay:** driving tutorial through real work: careful driving with a passenger (comfort rating), errands, the
  bank, the phone job board, traffic rules and cameras. Home scenes with Amma and Divya.
- **End hook:** asleep at 23:30, the phone buzzes once from an unknown number and goes quiet.

### Act 2, The accusation (Day 4)
- **Story:** police at the door at 08:15 and the arrest in front of the neighbours. Questioning at the Nagar Police
  Station: the inspector lays out E01, E02 and E03. Karthik's shock: "My phone was at the office!" Sundaram arrives.
- **Gameplay:** no driving: a tense interrogation with choices (stay calm, explain, demand a lawyer). Truthful,
  calm answers help the bail hearing.
- **End hook:** remand, his first night in a cell.

### Act 3, Isolation (Days 5–8)
- **Story:**
  - Two days in custody (a short playable jail routine), then bail with conditions: sign every evening 6–7 PM, no
    leaving the district (decided).
  - The office fires him, and Priya slaps him at the funeral gate.
  - Neighbours talk, Divya is bullied, and Amma stops going to the market.
  - Bala stands by him loudly ("I know you, da").
- **Gameplay:**
  - The bail rules start.
  - He has no salary, so the job board (deliveries, taxi) is his only income.
  - He learns the evening rhythm.
- **End hook:** at a signing, Constable Selvi slides back his pen with a note: "Ask the night watchman."

### Act 4, The investigation (Days 8–20)
- **Story:** the open middle. Karthik works the leads in any order:
  - **The watchman:** Muniyandi (E06), then the DVR copy (E07), which needs Divya's help to read.
  - **The alibi:** Ashwin and his doorbell video (E05).
  - **The road west:** Sekar's tea shop (E08) and the Pudhur Vayal farmers, angry at first.
  - **The family:** Priya. She finds the note (E09) with Revathi and starts to doubt. Meenakshi believes.
  - **The photos:** family albums (E14).
  - **The land:** the Collectorate's land records (E16) and the Siruvani Grand's doorman (E18).
  - **The time of death:** the post-mortem (E17), through Priya once she doubts.
  - **Meenakshi:** at Perur temple after the funeral, she lets him speak for the first time.
- **Gameplay:**
  - Evidence, the deduction board, relationships, journey reconstruction.
  - The bail signing shapes every day.
  - Suresh starts to watch him: a black sedan in the mirror, a "friendly" visit to Amma.
- **End hook:** the board points at Suresh, but nothing proves how he got the car.

### Act 5, The second twist (Days 20–21)
- **Story:** looking for how the key was copied, Karthik asks every key-cutter in the city, and the last place is
  his best friend's workshop. Bala's job card (E11). Bala understands what he did (E12) and breaks down. Karthik
  walks out.
- **Gameplay:** a choice that matters. Forgive him that night, or let him come back later on his own. Either way
  Bala testifies, but forgiving keeps him at Karthik's side in Act 6 (help at the court, a car when you need one).
- **End hook:** Suresh calls Karthik for the first time: "Boss. Let's talk. Just us."

### Act 6, The truth (Days 21–25)
- **Story:**
  - The meeting with Suresh is a trap. The player must stay lawful: don't take the bait, and call Sundaram.
  - Sundaram files for E10 and E13. Pandi tries to leave town from the Gandhipuram bus stand (E19) and is held; cornered,
    he turns approver. Joseph opens the books (E15), meeting Karthik at the trade fair complex, away from Suresh.
  - The trial: Karthik's journey reconstruction is played in court, minute by minute on the map.
  - Murugesan, convinced at last, arrests Suresh.
- **Ending:**
  - Meenakshi and Priya come to Karthik's house in Ganapathy. Priya apologises, and Meenakshi puts the keys and
    deeds of **The Mist Village** in Amma's hands: *"He trusted you. He'd want it to be yours."*
  - The bail conditions end. At dawn Karthik takes the scooter (or, with the family's gift, the white SUV) to
    Mettupalayam, and the ghat checkpoint lifts its barrier. Part 2 begins.

## 6b. Gatekeepers, bribes and frights (decided 2026-10-30)
The investigation isn't only finding people: it's getting past the ones who won't let him in, and holding his nerve.

### Gatekeepers: the honest way and the quick way
Every closed door has an honest way (slower, always there) and, where it's real life in this city, a bribe (fast).
**A bribe always works, and always costs later:** in court, Suresh's lawyer uses it to call that evidence bought
(it starts **disputed** and needs a second leg), the inspector's suspicion rises if he hears of it, and Sundaram is
disappointed ("A fact you paid for walks on one leg, thambi"). Refusing a bribe someone asks for never costs
anything but time. Karthik never bribes the police.

| Where | Who blocks him | The honest way | The quick way, and what it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raghavan Developers' building, night | **Muniyandi** at the gate: *"Police said nobody goes up. And you're the accused!"* He shuts the gate in his face | Come back on a later night with hot tea and vadai, sit with him; ask about his son who's out of work. On the third night he talks (E06), and in Act 4 sends Karthik to his nephew (E07) | ₹500 "for tea": he lets him into the lobby and talks that night. In court E06 is disputed until E07 backs it |
| The DVR copy, the nephew's house | **The nephew** wants ₹5,000 for "his" copy | Divya's college friends show it's the building's own footage, and Sundaram asks for it properly; the nephew hands it over | Pay: the copy comes at once, but its chain of custody is weak and the defence attacks it |
| District Collectorate, patta counter | **A clerk**: *"Apply online. Fifteen working days."* A tout by the door offers *"Two thousand, today itself"* | Apply (the game's clock runs the 15 days while he works other leads), or Revathi's office copy of the file (Act 4) | Pay the tout: E16 the same day, but the tout is later caught and Karthik's name is in his book: suspicion rises |
| Government Hospital, mortuary | **The attendant** hints that a report "can be seen" for money | Only through Priya, once she doubts (Act 4): she asks as the family | Refused in any case: the attendant has nothing real to sell (a fake copy, which Sundaram spots) |
| The Siruvani Grand | **Security** at the lobby door: a man in a delivery uniform doesn't get past | Buy a good shirt at a mall and have lunch there (₹1,450): the doorman Ganesan talks to a guest (E18) | A tip to the doorman at the gate: he takes it and says nothing useful |
| Pudhur Vayal | **Ponnusamy and the farmers** surround his scooter: they think he stole their money | Come back with Raghavan's note (E09) and show them; they become his witnesses | (none: they can't be bought, and offering makes them angrier) |
| Bala's workshop, late night | **The shutter's down**; Bala doesn't answer his phone | Wait till morning | (none) |

| Act 6, the trap | An empty mill shed on the Mettupalayam road | Suresh's "just us" meeting. The shed's lights go off; Pandi's step on the gravel behind. If Karthik called Sundaram and the police first, Selvi's torch comes through the door; if he came alone, he must run for the scooter |

**Rule:** a fright never kills or injures Karthik and never ends the game. It costs stress and sometimes time, and
it tells the player they're getting close.

## 7. Relationships (shown through behaviour, not numbers)
- **Amma:** trust stays, health worsens with gossip. Coming home for dinner, not missing signings and paying rent
  keep her steady.
- **Divya:** starts teasing, turns angry when bullied, then becomes his tech partner in Act 4 if he includes her.
- **Bala:** loyal, then broken in Act 5. The forgive-or-wait choice sets the end of Act 6.
- **Priya:** hate, then doubt (Act 4), then ally. Being honest with her and returning her father's diary moves her.
- **Inspector Murugesan:** suspicion is the bail meter. Lates and strikes raise it; truthful answers and on-time
  signings lower it.
- **Selvi:** quiet help, if Karthik is always polite and never asks her to break rules.
- **Suresh:** smiles, then pressure, then a trap. He never threatens openly in front of witnesses.
